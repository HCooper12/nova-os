Shopping and Stash, Instruments in full

Shopping keeps all of direction A, then learns your meals and the price of each line at Coles and Woolworths. The Stash shows each link's picture, sends anything to your list, and asks you to check a bottle's level a week before it runs out.

Every price on this page is an invented example. So is every product, recipe, link, date and count. Nova does not read any supermarket price today; where prices could honestly come from, and what that costs, is your first call at the foot of the page. Product pictures are drawn for the mockup; Nova would show each page's own picture. The research is design/audits/redesign-2026-09/14-shopping-stash-r2-research.md.

Where the prices could come from

Read in October 2026; the research file has the sources. Today no supermarket in Australia offers a sanctioned price feed. The ACCC recommended in February 2025 that Coles and Woolworths open one to comparison tools; the government agreed in principle, and I found no sign one exists yet.

Woolworths

Prices and nutrition, against its terms

Its website answers product searches from its own service, nutrition panel included. Its terms forbid "any robot, spider, site search and retrieval application or other mechanism to retrieve or index any portion of the Site".

Online prices, usually national unless a store is chosen. Specials change each Wednesday.

Coles

Prices behind a bot wall, against its terms

Sits behind a bot check that refused cloud addresses outright on 16 September. Its terms forbid copying or reproducing any of its content. One comparison app links out to Coles instead, saying Coles does not permit automated lookups.

Aldi

Prices yes, nutrition no, no online shop

Its site lists every product with a price and a unit price, but shows no nutrition to a visitor and sells nothing online. Its terms allow search engines and forbid any other automated access. So Aldi can never get a "better on both" suggestion from Aldi's own data.

Open Food Facts

Macros, openly licensed

Nova already reads it by barcode for scans and for the Pick it up finder. Many Australian products are in it, with the nutrition panel per 100 g. This is the clean source for the macro half of "better on both".

The restock rhythm

Nova cannot see inside the bottle. It counts days from when you bought it, and asks you to look before it would run out.

The check rides the reminders Nova already keeps: a notification to your phone that waits for quiet hours to end, and a Telegram line if you use it. Tapping it opens this item with three answers. Ticking it off on the shopping list later counts as bought, so the loop closes on its own. After two real cycles Nova proposes a truer rhythm from what you did; it never changes one by itself.

Every write, and its undo

Each change lands at once and answers with Undo in the island for four seconds; the ones marked new need a server change, named in the research.

  • QuantityThe stepper's other button; Undo puts back the number before.
  • Remove one linenew routeUndo restores the line exactly, through the restore route that exists.
  • Clear what you got, clear everythingUndo restores the exact items (exists today for clear all).
  • Add a dish's missing lines, a recipe, the weekundo is newUndo removes exactly the lines that were added, by their ids.

  • Stash a linkpicture is newUndo removes the line just written.

On the Mac, for the desktop round

Shopping would hold one reading width for the list, with Your meals and the basket card in a column beside it rather than above. The price sheet becomes a popover from the price cell. Arrow keys move, Space ticks, Delete removes, Command-Z undoes.

Stash grids widen to four cards a row; the shelf bar becomes a sidebar of shelves with their counts, and search takes Command-F.

Your calls

  1. Where prices come fromNo sanctioned source exists, so every option has a cost. (a) Nova reads the public product pages of Woolworths and Coles once a week, after Wednesday's change, only for lines on your list or pinned, from your Mac: this is against the letter of both chains' terms, and either can block it at any time; when one does, its prices disappear and the screen says so. (b) Nova reads no supermarket: the price cells and totals do not appear, the kinds and macros still do (from Open Food Facts), and each kind links out so you can check the price yourself. (c) Build (b) now and switch to a supermarket's own price feed the day one exists. Whichever you choose, nothing shows a price Nova did not read that week.
  2. AldiYes: if you chose (a), Nova reads Aldi's listed prices too, with the same terms problem, but without nutrition, so Aldi joins the price comparison and never the "better on both". No: Aldi stays dashed and named, with a link that opens its site for you to look yourself.
  3. What "better on macros" meansProposed: at least as much protein per 100 g and no more kilojoules per 100 g, and cheaper per kilo, all three at once, so a suggestion is never a trade you did not ask for. Yes: that rule. No: you name the one you want (less fat, more protein per dollar), and code uses that instead.
  4. An "Order online" group on the shopping listYes: things sent from the Stash keep their link and picture in their own group at the foot of the list; it adds one group to the list's format, and every reader of the list changes with it. No: they join Household and other, as plain lines without their link.
  5. Where a restock rhythm is keptYes, in the vault: the Stash line carries it, readable and editable in Obsidian ("lasts:: 8 weeks, bought:: 12 Sep"), a change to the Stash file's format that every reader and writer takes together. No: Nova keeps it in its own data on the Mac; the Stash file stays as it is, and the rhythm is lost if that data is.

Yes, above: the rotation is one glance from the top, and in the shop you scroll once more.
