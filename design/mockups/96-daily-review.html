Part 2

The schedule, as code applies it

Seven widening gaps. The first five are the Library's table already pinned in twins.test.js; the two long steps keep a vault this size from drowning the morning. Your answer is the only thing that moves a page: being shown it does not.

// server/lib/conceptReview.js (proposed). Days are local
// calendar dates as strings: the Repertoire's AEST lesson.
export const GAPS = [1, 3, 7, 16, 35, 90, 180];   // days
export const DAILY = 5;           // at most five a day
export const NEW_WHEN_BELOW = 3;  // one new page on a lighter day

// one tap after reading moves the next date
export function answer(card, grade, today) {
  const step =
    grade === 'got'   ? Math.min(card.step + 1, GAPS.length - 1)
  : grade === 'fuzzy' ? card.step          // same gap again
  :                     0;                  // forgot: tomorrow
  return { ...card, step, last: today,
           due: shiftDate(today, GAPS[step]) };
}
// a new page has step 0: Got it sends it 3 days out,
// Fuzzy or Forgot bring it back tomorrow.
// No answer, no move: it stays due and climbs the queue.

// which surfaces first: the most overdue for its gap
export function queue(cards, today) {
  const risk = (c) => daysBetween(c.last, today) / GAPS[c.step];
  const due = cards.filter((c) => c.due && c.due <= today)
    .sort((a, b) => risk(b) - risk(a)       // furthest past its gap
                 || a.step - b.step          // then the most fragile
                 || a.title.localeCompare(b.title));
  const day = due.slice(0, DAILY);
  if (due.length < NEW_WHEN_BELOW) {
    const fresh = nextNew(cards);           // newest source first
    if (fresh) day.push(fresh);
  }
  return day;
}

Try the ladder

A demo page on step 2 (its next gap is 7 days). Each tap is one review.

  • New pages enter newest source first. A concept from the podcast you finished on Thursday gets its first look the next morning, when the curve is steepest. Older pages enter behind them, the most linked first, or the moment you add one from its page in Notes.
  • When several are due, the one furthest past its own gap leads: three days late on a 3 day gap outranks three days late on a 35 day gap.
  • A missed morning costs nothing. Nothing moves until you answer; the page waits at the head of tomorrow's queue.

Part 3 · Your Home

The card on the summary Home

On your phone it is a moment: at the head of Home while something is due, gone once the day's reviews are answered. Tap the buttons in the phone; every answer is acted out where it lands.

Three pages are due this morning. The first was last seen on Thursday.

The concept, in the serif

The head of the server's queue (Part 2). The pick moves to the Mac, so the date hash and its twin in the morning brief are deleted: both read one answer.

The gist

The page's cached summary when one exists, else its first paragraph, clamped to three lines. A skeleton while it arrives; the card never says "Summarizing".

The source, only where the page records it

The first Source page named in its sources: line: that page's title, and a kind word only from what the page says (a podcast tag, or the url: YouTube reads as video). A time shows only when it sits on the same line as the source link. "+1" when the page names more than one.

Connected, in the Galaxy's colours

Up to three of the page's own wikilinks: pages that link back first, then the most linked. Gold concept, violet topic, coral person. Each opens that page.

The curve

Dots are your answers, on their dates, in their colours. The line between them is the forgetting idea drawn to the schedule's gaps; Nova measures nothing about your memory, so it carries no percentage.

Three buttons that say what they do

Each shows where the page goes: Got it, 7 days. One tap files the answer on the Inbox rails with Undo; the next page waits behind Next, so you decide when to move on.

Part 7

The shuffle, and the name

The reel stays, after the reviews

A shuffle on a due card would swap the page the schedule chose for a random one, and the one you skipped would quietly lose its day. So the reel leaves the due card.

It returns where it cannot break the order: once the day's reviews are done, Draw one early spins your pages that have never been reviewed and lands on one. That page enters as new, with its first look now, and is scheduled from your answer like any other. Same SpinReveal, same chime, and the draw is real.

Two things called Daily review

The Home card keeps Daily review: it is the name you used on 11 October. The evening model read of your day becomes Day read wherever it is shown (the Inbox loop, Operations, the Journal label).

The label already on disk, "Daily review reflection", is read by the journal's author map, commitments and the weekly debrief, so the files keep it and only the display changes. Changing the stored label would mean changing every reader at once.

Your calls

  1. The gaps: 1, 3, 7, 16, 35, 90, 180 days Yes: two long steps after the Library's five; about 120 pages a year get a schedule at three or four cards a morning. No, keep the Library's five: the two schedules stay identical, and about 87 pages a year enter at the same load, because every learned page keeps returning every 35 days.
  2. A morning holds up to five, with one new page on a lighter day Yes: two minutes or so a day; anything past five waits for tomorrow, most overdue first. Larger (say ten): more of the vault enters, and the moment becomes a short session of five to eight minutes.
  3. New pages enter newest source first Yes: what you heard this week gets reviewed while it is fresh; the 281 already there enter slowly, most linked first, or when you add one from Notes. No, the backlog first: the older pages get their turn sooner, and this week's podcast waits weeks for its first review.
  4. Where your answers live: a log in your vault, the schedule worked out from it Yes: one line per answer appended to Wiki/Library/Review Log.md (like the Repertoire Log), readable in Obsidian; the schedule is rebuilt from it into server/data, so nothing is lost if that folder is rebuilt. No, server data only (as the Library does): nothing new in your vault, and a rebuild of server data starts every page over as new.
  5. Read first, then answer Yes (drawn): the gist shows at once and you answer after reading. Recall first: the card shows only the title until you tap to reveal; harder, and trying to recall before reading is what makes things stick best. One extra tap a card.
  6. The evening read is shown as "Day read" Yes: "Daily review" means only this card, everywhere you look; the files on disk keep their label. No: both keep the name, and a card about a podcast concept and Nova's evening coaching keep sharing it in the Inbox, Ops and the Journal.
  7. The shuffle reel only as "Draw one early" Yes: the reel and its chime stay, after the day's reviews, and every draw enters the schedule. Remove it: one less control; pulling a page in early is done from its page in Notes.
