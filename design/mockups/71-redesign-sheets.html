Sheets, menus and the island

Everything that sits on top of a page: the sheet you pull down, the menu you hold for, the row you swipe, the island that drops a notice. Today they come in six looks with fourteen kinds of dim, and five of the sheets ignore your finger. Three ways to make them one material with one set of physics.

Demo content only: the meals, routines, figures and times are invented. Every "Today" behaviour on this page was measured in the app on 5 October, in demo mode; the audit has the numbers.

X1 · A spring, or the curve

Both sheets below have the rubber band on the right side. The only difference is what happens after you let go. Pull each a little way and flick it back up, or throw it down and catch it before it leaves. The line under each phone says how fast you let go and how fast the sheet's first frame moved.

My recommendation: yes to springs, no to the library. Nova already ships one. The island's integrator in islandCore.js keeps its velocity through every re-aim, is tested by the server suite, and already drives every notice the island shows. Lifted into its own module it can carry the sheets, the swipe rows and the hold menu with no new dependency. Apple's sheet spring (damping 0.8, response 0.3 s) is a 400 ms, 0.8 spring in its terms. Entrances stay on the curve. The curve's real cost is the seam: an ease-out started at the finger's average speed leaves at 2.25 times it.

X2 · Exits, everywhere or not

Today 18 of the 36 overlays in the app animate out when closed with a button, none of the 9 sheets lets go of its dim when thrown, and 9 that live on their own history entry are followed by a whole-page cross-fade after they close. Close both with Done, or throw both, ideally at quarter speed.

Today

Done: a 12 point fall with the dim, then the whole page, tab bar included, is photographed and cross-faded. A throw, as measured on the recipe sheet: the dim holds at full strength, the sheet stands back up for a frame, then the page cross-fades.

One rule: leave the way you came

Every way out uses the path it came in by, with its dim, and nothing else on the page moves. Under Reduce Motion the same moment is a 200 ms cross-fade.

My recommendation: yes, everywhere, as one rule. Every overlay leaves by the path it came, its dim leaves with it, on every way out (Done, a tap on the dim, Escape, a throw, Back), and no second page-wide transition follows. Under Reduce Motion, a 200 ms cross-fade, which today's global rule removes.

X5 · A haptic on a flick: test it on your phone

On your installed Nova, code cannot make your phone tick. The one path iOS leaves a web app is a real switch under your own finger, which is how Nova's buttons tick on your phone today. This grabber has a real, invisible switch over it. Open this page on your iPhone and try both.

Two tries, one minute

1. Tap the grabber once. You should feel a tick. If you do not, this page cannot test anything on this phone, and the answer waits for the native shell.

2. Flick the sheet down by the grabber. If you feel a tick as it goes, a flick haptic can be built on your phone today. If you feel nothing, it cannot, and it waits for the native shell (X10).

Nothing tried yet.

My recommendation: no for sheets. Apple's own sheets do not tick when thrown away, so a tick there would be Nova's invention, and the most likely result of try 2 is nothing. Keep the "threshold" word for the swipe row's full-swipe line, where iOS Mail does tick, and let the native shell deliver it. If try 2 surprises us, say so and I will revisit.

Two attempts at a fade under the top bar broke the bar on your phone, both times through a separate blurred layer over it. A sheet has the same edge, where its list meets its fixed header, and is self-contained. Scroll both lists.
