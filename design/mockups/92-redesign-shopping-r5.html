Orange for Everyday Rewards, blue for Flybuys, and points you can tell apart

Each offer card now wears its programme's colour as a faint wash on Nova's glass, with the programme's mark at its top, so you know whose offer it is before you read a word. A rate like 10× is an orchid pill; an amount like 1,000 pts is a violet number; a multiplier offer shows both.

Every offer, multiplier, end date and spend figure here is invented, as are round 3's prices. None is a real current offer. The marks are not the programmes' logos: each is the brand colour and the programme's name, drawn here; no logo artwork or file was copied. Nova reads no loyalty account and never will.

WCA Store tags, unchanged

A store tag is a tinted letter and means the shop: where a price was read, where the pick is. A programme mark is a filled tile and a name, and means the programme. They sit side by side on an offer sheet ("W Woolworths · personalised to you") and never stand in for each other.

The mark is mine, not theirs: their colour, Nova's points glyph, and the name in the system face. A real logo would need the official file from each programme (your call 1).

Two kinds of points

A rate and an amount were the same violet in round 4. Now each has its own colour and its own form, everywhere points appear: the list line, the card, the callout, the sheet, the total.

10×

A rate. Points for every dollar. A filled orchid pill, rounded heavy type, dark ink on the colour. It says how fast points come, not how many.

1,000pts

An amount. Points you actually get. A violet rounded number with a small "pts", no fill. Totals and bonuses are always this.

10×30pts

Both, on one card. A multiplier offer shows the rate, then what it comes to on what you are buying: 10× on $3.00 of frozen peas is 30 pts. Code does the sum, never a model.

Neither is red, which means Nova pushing back; green still means a saving (the "about $5.00" beside a figure), and gold still means waiting or going off. The two differ in form as well as hue, so they stay apart for colour-blind eyes.

Where an offer can come from

Two kinds of offer reach Nova by different doors. The research, with its sources, is in 14-shopping-rewards-research.md.

Open to every member

Gift-card weeks (10x or 20x points) and catalogue bonus-point items. They are printed in the weekly Coles and Woolworths catalogues and on in-store posters, most need no activation, and Coles' gift-card weeks are not shown in the Flybuys app at all. Nova can know these today from a catalogue email you pass on. Reading a catalogue or a points-deals feed by itself is against those sites' terms, which is your call 1.

Personalised to you

Everyday Rewards boosts and Flybuys offers. Chosen from your own shopping, shown in the apps, on the websites and in their emails, and they only count once you press Boost or Activate before you shop. Nova knows one when you pass it on: an email your Mail rule saves, or a screenshot you send with Send to Nova.

Not possible

Signing in to either programme, pressing Boost or Activate, seeing your points balance, or reading the apps' notifications (iOS gives a Shortcut no access to another app's notifications). Neither programme offers a public API.

How code matches and values an offer

A model may read an email or a screenshot into a record. Code then checks every field, and nothing reaches the list that code could not check.

// an offer is a record with every field filled, or it is not shown
offer = { programme, shop, on, points or multiplier, ends, activate: yes|no, source, readAt }

// it shows only when it matches something real
match = a line on the list               // by kind name; an unsure match asks you once
     or something you buy often           // ticked off 4+ times in 8 weeks: your call 5
     or a shop you already spend at       // gift cards only, from Money's bank lines: your call 6

worth   = points × $10 / 2,000              // both programmes redeem 2,000 points for $10
ends    = the offer's own end date; the morning after, it is dropped and one line says so
pick    = the price cell stays cash; the sheet says what the points change // your call 4

Further refinements, each drawn once

  • The barScroll the phone: the large title folds into a small one in the bar, and the bar's material appears only once content runs under it. At the top there is no bar at all.
  • Touch downA row highlights the instant your finger lands and fades on release, so a tap is felt before anything changes.
  • Ticked lines recedeA ticked name drops to regular weight and 40% ink, and its price fades, so what is left stands out.
  • Numbers arriveThe ring's count and the points total count up from zero when the screen opens (your 9 Oct call), and arrive set under reduced motion.
  • Two hues for pointsRound 4 used one violet for every offer. Round 5 splits it: orchid pills for rates, violet numbers for amounts, and the programme's own colour for whose offer it is. Green still means money saved, gold still means food that goes off.

The pack rule, as code applies it

No model decides how many to buy. The need is read from the recipes behind the line, by the same parseAmount that scales a recipe today (src/recipeScale.js), so "½ brown onion" in the chilli is a need of 0.5. Code then prices every way to buy at least that much and picks one.

// need: the sum of every recipe amount behind this line, scaled to the servings planned
need = sum(line.needs.map(n => n.qty))          // ½ + 2 + ½ = 3 onions

// every way to buy it, at every shop with a price read this week
for each option (single, bag of 5, 4-pack, 1 kg ...):
  count = ceil(need / option.size)              // you cannot buy half a bag
  total = count × option.price
  spare = count × option.size − need

cheapest = lowest total (ties: less spare)
exact    = least spare (ties: lower total)

// the waste guard: a pack whose spares go off before you use them
if cheapest.spare > exact.spare
   and shelfLife(line) < 14 days and it does not freeze
   and no other meal this week uses the spare
   and exact.total − cheapest.total < $0.50:       // your call 3 sets this margin
  pick = exact                                   // "saves 20c, but 2 would go off"
else
  pick = cheapest

How long things keep: code's table

The "keeps" judgement on each line comes from a small table, by kind, not from a model. Gold marks the ones that go off inside two weeks, where the waste guard applies. These are my estimates for a Melbourne kitchen, stored as code with the list; your call 5 is whether you can edit them in the vault.

Where each price comes from, unchanged since round 2

No supermarket in Australia offers an approved price feed yet. The research is in 14-shopping-stash-r2-research.md. What changed this round is how a price is shown, not where it comes from.

WWoolworths and CColes

Prices are on their public sites, and both sites' terms forbid automated reading. A narrow weekly read from your Mac, just after Wednesday's specials change, is possible and blockable. Every price shows the date it was read; one older than the next Wednesday would say "last week's".

AAldi

Prices are listed on its site, with no nutrition and no online shop, and its terms forbid automated access too. When not read, Aldi stays a dashed letter, named, in the basket, and is never claimed cheapest.

·Why not daily

A daily price means reading the chains every day, which their terms forbid. Specials change once a week, on Wednesday, so a Wednesday read is about as fresh as their own catalogues. Each price cell says the date of its read, so you can always see how old it is.

    What it needs that does not exist

    • The need, kept per recipeA list line carries one amount and one source today (server/lib/shoppingList.js). Summing ½ from the chilli and 2 from a stew needs a needs list on the line, each with its recipe and amount. That changes the list's frontmatter, so shoppingList.js, mealPrep.js, the inbox's shopping route and valsMisc.js change together (your call 4).
    • Pack sizes per productEach product a line matches carries its size in the line's unit (a 1 kg onion bag counts as about 5 onions). Code reads the size from the product's own name; a bag sold by weight states its count as "about", and the sheet says so.
    • The shelf-life tableA small pure module beside recipeScale.js, with its own tests: kind to days, and whether it freezes.
    • An offers recordA small store in server/data (operational, rebuilt from what arrives; offers expire within weeks), each record with programme, shop, what it is on, points or multiplier, end date, activation, source and when it was read. Code validates every field; a model only reads an email or a screenshot into it.
    • A way in for emailsNone exists: server/lib has no mail reader. Proposed: a Mail rule on your Mac (Mail would need your Outlook account) that saves Everyday Rewards and Flybuys emails to a folder Nova watches. No password reaches Nova.
    • Screenshots through Send to NovaThe share-sheet Shortcut takes text and links today (docs/iphone-shortcuts.md), and a photo sent on Telegram is read as food. Offers need an image door and a route that reads an offer, not a meal.
    • A bought log and a merchant matchThe list forgets a line once it is cleared, so "you buy it often" needs a log of ticks (call 5). Matching a gift-card brand to your spending needs Money to name merchants, not just categories (call 6).
    • PricesYour call 2. Nothing here works without a source except the need and the waste judgement, which still show on every line with no prices at all.

    1. The marks, or the real logos?Drawn: each programme is its brand colour in a small tile with Nova's points glyph, and its name in the system face. If you want the actual Everyday Rewards and Flybuys logos instead, they are trademarks and there is no licensed file here: it would need the official asset from each programme's brand team (or their media kit), and permission to use it in your own app. Keep the marks, and it ships as drawn. Ask for the logos, and the mark stays until you have the files.

How strong the programme colour isDrawn: a faint wash from the top corner (about a fifth strength, fading out by three quarters across) and an edge highlight, on the same glass as every Nova card.
