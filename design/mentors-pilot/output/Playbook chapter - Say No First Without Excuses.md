# Say No First Without Excuses

Decline a request or invitation with the no first, a thank you and a touch of kindness, and no justification unless one is asked for and earned.

## When to use it
- A boss or coworker asks for something you do not want to do or cannot take on.
- You are turning down an invitation from a friend or acquaintance.
- A sales pitch or an unwanted offer needs to be stopped.
- You are about to write a long message full of reasons.
## The method
1. Check your gut. If it says no, the decision is made, so do not move it.
2. Say the no first, with no excuse, for example I can't be there or No, thank you.
3. Add gratitude for the invitation and for being thought of.
4. Add a little kindness, such as wishing them a great time and asking them to tell you how it went.
5. Stop. Let them handle the disappointment and hold the silence instead of softening.
6. Give a reason only if they ask and the person matters to you, and keep it short and true.
7. In a message, name the disappointment instead of apologizing at length.
## Rules
- If you want to say no, your gut has already decided and you control only how you say it. (stated, S1 14:37)
- The decline comes first with no excuses or justifications, because justifications are over nice. (stated, S1 15:42)
- At work, extra reasons are just sounding nice. (stated, S1 15:42)
- A short legitimate reason is acceptable for a close friend who understands, such as picking up the kids. (evidenced, S1 16:17; needs: a close friend who understands)
- Offer reasons only if they ask and the person actually matters to you. (stated, S1 18:38)
- The three parts of a no for an invitation are the no, gratitude and a little kindness, after which you let it go. (stated, S2 18:20)
- In a message, say that the no will probably disappoint them, instead of a long apology about being busy. (stated, S2 08:37)
- To show you weighed the decision, say you have thought about it. (stated, S2 16:46)
- To interrupt a pitch you do not want, say so cheerfully after a few seconds of listening. (evidenced, S2 16:46; needs: a pitch you do not want and a calm, cheerful tone)
- After saying no, resist filling the silence with adjustments or with checking whether you met their expectations. (stated, S2 06:26)
## Phrases to reuse
- No, thank you.
- I can't make it. I can't do it. Not this time.
- I'm making a promise to myself on my priorities.
- I've thought about it, and I have to say no.
- I need to say no.
- I need to stop you right here.
- It's not in my plans right now.
- No, thank you, I appreciate that.
- I'm going to have to disappoint you.
- Hey, I know this will probably disappoint you. I won't be able to come.
## Mistakes to avoid
- Opening with regret, such as Oh, I really wish I could.
- Listing how busy and stressed you are.
- Apologizing at length in a message.
- Backfilling the silence after the no with adjustments.
- Moving the decision after your gut has made it.
## Where it stops applying
- The notes do not say what to do if the person keeps pressing after the no.
- A true, short reason is allowed for a close friend who understands, and for someone who asks and matters to you.
- He points to an earlier episode on how to properly say no, which the notes do not describe.
